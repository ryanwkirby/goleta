# How to play goleta

**The original written rules of goleta, in plain English.**

This is the game as it is played at a table with real cards, written for
somebody who has never played it. It is not a description of this app —
[`RULES.md`](RULES.md) is that, and its last section says where the two differ
and why. Learn the game here; settle an argument about the software there.

---

## The short version

Four to eight players. One deck. Everybody's hand face up on the table where
everyone can see it.

**You win by being the last player still holding cards.** Run out and you are
out.

**If you can play a card, you must.** No passing, no holding back, no choosing
to draw instead.

That is the whole game. Everything below is detail.

## Why it feels backwards

goleta began as Crazy Eights and was changed, over years, to make it play
faster. The change that matters is that the goal was turned around.

In Crazy Eights you race to get rid of your cards. Here, getting rid of your
last one knocks you out — so cards are the thing keeping you in, and every card
you play is a step towards the door. Since playing is compulsory, **a turn where
you can play is a turn you lose ground on.**

Drawing is the only way to gain a card, and you are only allowed to draw when
you are stuck. So being stuck is good, and being thoroughly stuck is the best
turn you can have.

Which means this is not a game about playing well. It is about arranging a hand
you *can't* play — and watching everybody else closely enough to know whether
they are doing the same.

## Setting up

Pick a dealer. The deal moves one seat clockwise each round, so the same person
does not open every game.

The dealer gives out cards **face up**, one at a time, clockwise, taking their
own last, until everybody has **three**. Every hand stays face up and visible
for the whole game. You are meant to see what everyone is holding.

Put the rest of the deck face down in the middle. Turn its top card over and lay
it beside the deck — that is the **seed card**, and it is what the first player
has to match.

The player to the dealer's left goes first, and play moves clockwise from there.

## Your turn

Look at the face-up card in the middle. You may play any card from your hand
that **matches it by suit or by rank** — a spade on a spade, a seven on a seven.

Put it face up on the pile, and your turn is over.

**If you hold a card that matches, you have to play it.** If you hold several,
you choose which. What you may not do is pass, or draw instead.

### Eights are wild

An 8 plays on top of anything. When you play one you **name the suit** everybody
has to match from then on — say it out loud — and your turn is over.

An 8 is not a card you want. Because it is always playable, and because playing
is compulsory, **you can never legally draw while you are holding one**: it shuts
the best turn in the game off until it is gone, and when it goes, that is a card
you no longer have. Naming the suit is what you get back for it.

### If you can't play, you draw

Take one card off the deck into your hand. Then:

- **If it gives you a play, you must play it**, and your turn ends.
- **If it doesn't, draw again** — up to **three cards** in one turn.
- **If all three leave you stuck**, your turn is over and the next player goes.
  You keep all three.

Three draws means you were genuinely stuck the whole way through, and you come
out of it three cards richer. That is as well as a turn goes.

### If the deck runs out

Take the face-up pile, shuffle it, and set it back down face down as the new
deck. Turn its top card over to start the pile again, and carry on.

If that happened part-way through your draws, keep going — you can still take
your three, so long as you still cannot play. But the newly turned card is a new
card to match, and if it hands you a play, you play it.

## The Sunny Rule

**You may only touch the deck when you have nothing to play.**

Drawing is the reward for being stuck. Reaching for the deck when you had a card
all along is helping yourself to a reward you did not earn, and it is the one
thing in this game that counts as cheating.

If you have a playable card and you reach out and touch the face-down deck
anyway, as if to draw, any other player may call **"Sunny Rule!"** on you.

**If nobody calls it, nothing happens.** You got away with it and play carries on
as normal. Nobody is watching on your behalf — the rule only exists when somebody
at the table notices and says so out loud. Every hand being face up is what makes
that possible, and it is most of why the cards are dealt that way.

If somebody does call it, you do three things, in this order:

1. **Play the card you should have played** — normally, exactly as if you had
   played it in the first place.
2. **Play a second card as punishment.** Any card left in your hand, your choice.
   It does not have to match anything.
3. **Turn over the card you touched.** It goes face up on the pile and becomes
   the new card everybody matches.

Your turn is then over.

So being caught costs you two cards where an honest turn would have cost one,
and it hands the table a new card in play that you did not choose. Getting away
with it gains you a card you had no right to.

## Going out, and winning

When your last card goes to the middle, you are out. You leave the game and it
carries on without you.

That keeps happening until one player is left holding cards. **They win.**

---

**A note on eights turned up off the deck.** If the seed card at the start is an
8, it is a **natural 8**: it counts as an ordinary card of the suit printed on
it, and nobody names anything. An 8 is only wild when it is played out of
somebody's hand.
